How it works

From a dump to an app in three steps

The Switch and every Apple Silicon Mac run ARM64. Napple keeps the game's own machine code and rewrites, once, only what macOS does not allow. The runtime then answers the console's system calls and translates its graphics API to Metal.

  1. Add your game

    Open Napple Studio, add your own prod.keys once, and drop your dump on the window: XCI, NSP, NSZ or XCZ, with updates and DLC merged in.

  2. Build

    Studio decrypts, analyses and recompiles ahead of time, then packages a standalone, locally signed .app. Progress and a live log are right there.

  3. Play

    Launch it from the library. Plug in a controller, open the in-game overlay with F1 and tune the picture, speed and controls to taste.

Features

Made to be played, not just booted

Everything below runs locally on your Mac. Per-title results vary while Napple is in beta; see compatibility for what has been tried.

Local play

A native app per game, launched from your library. Direct execution of the game's own code, with no interpreter and no JIT at run time.

LAN and split-screen

Play a game's own local multiplayer on one Mac with several controllers, or over your local network with friends in the same room. No internet needed.

Metal renderer

The console's NVN graphics API and its Maxwell shaders are translated to Metal, with a pipeline cache on disk so the second run is smoother.

Upscaling and FPS unlock

Render scale above native, supersampling and an upscaler choice (MetalFX spatial where available, edge-adaptive or CAS). Unlocked frame rates are done per title so game speed does not change.

Controllers, amiibo, Mii

Game controllers with rumble, plus keyboard and mouse. Virtual amiibo and an IR camera through your webcam, and Mii data, are available where a game uses them.

Mods

Install community .napmod files: patches, cheats, replacement files and per-title settings. Mods are never downloaded for you, and code patches stay off until you turn them on.

Saves stay local

Saves live in ~/Library/Application Support/Napple, in plain folders you can back up, copy or delete yourself. There is no cloud and no sync.

The in-game overlay

Press F1 (or the controller's Home button) in any running game. Everything is changed live, and per game.

  • Audio / Video: render scale, upscaler, supersampling, volume.
  • FPS / Speed: frame-rate limit or unlock, game speed.
  • Controls: map controllers and the keyboard.
  • Game / System: amiibo, the IR camera and system options.

Privacy

Nothing leaves your Mac

Napple has no accounts, no servers of its own, no cloud saves, no friends list and no telemetry. There is nothing to sign in to and nothing to opt out of.

What stays on your Mac

  • Your games and keys. They are read at build time, from your disk.
  • Your saves and settings. Plain files in your Application Support folder.
  • Your play. No usage data, crash reports or identifiers are collected.

What Napple does not have

  • No Napple account, sign-in or profile.
  • No online play service, relay rooms or matchmaking.
  • No analytics, ads or trackers, here or in the app.

The network is used only when you ask for it: LAN play with people on your own network, downloading a release from GitHub, or fetching the optional public amiibo list. Read the full privacy notice.

Set up in a few minutes

  1. Open the .dmg and drag Napple Studio to Applications.
  2. Open it. On first launch Studio sets up the engine for you.
  3. Add your own prod.keys (and firmware for the system fonts) once.
  4. Drop a game you dumped yourself, press build, then play.

Prefer a terminal? The napple CLI does every stage:

napple build game.xci update.nsp -k prod.keys --firmware firmware.zip -o ~/Apps

See the documentation for the CLI, mods, and the architecture.

"macOS can't verify this app"?

Beta builds are signed ad hoc unless the maintainers have notarisation set up. Right-click the app and choose Open, or run once:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/NappleStudio.app

Games you build are signed locally on your Mac, for you.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly

Is this legal? Does it include games?

Napple includes no games, no keys, no firmware and no Nintendo code, and it never downloads any. You must own the console and the games and supply your own legitimately dumped files. Do not use it for piracy, and do not share the apps it builds: they contain content derived from your copy. Napple is not affiliated with Nintendo.

Is it an emulator?

No. Napple is a toolchain. The game's own ARM64 code is rewritten ahead of time and runs directly on your CPU, with no interpreter and no JIT. The runtime re-implements the console's system services and translates its graphics to Metal.

Can I play online?

No. Online play and accounts are not part of Napple, and it does not connect to any console service. What works is local: one Mac with several controllers, and LAN play between Macs on the same network, in games that offer those modes.

Where are my saves?

On your disk, in ~/Library/Application Support/Napple, one folder per title. Nothing is uploaded and there is no cloud sync, so back them up like any other files.

Which games work?

It varies by game and is changing fast. The compatibility page lists each tested game and its status per stage, including what is partial or not yet tried. It is reported by the developers on their own Macs, not guaranteed.

What about iPhone and iPad?

iOS and iPadOS builds are in development and not part of this beta release. The Mac comes first.

How do I report a problem?

Open an issue on GitHub (see how to report). Never attach game files, keys or firmware.
