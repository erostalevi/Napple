Privacy

Updated: 2026-11-13. Not legal advice.

The short version: Napple collects no data. The app and the command-line tool have no accounts, no servers of their own, no analytics and no crash reporting, and they do not send anything about you or your games to the Napple project.

The Napple app

  • Your games, keys and firmware are read from your disk when you build, and are not uploaded anywhere.
  • Saves, settings, virtual amiibo and Mii data are plain files under ~/Library/Application Support/Napple. They stay on your Mac. Back them up or delete them yourself.
  • There is no Napple account, sign-in, cloud save, friends list or online play service.

When the network is used

Only when you do something that needs it:

  • LAN play: traffic between your Mac and other players on your own local network, in games that have a LAN or local multiplayer mode.
  • Downloads: getting a release from GitHub, or installing an engine version from GitHub releases in Studio, contacts GitHub, which has its own privacy statement.
  • Amiibo list: the optional amiibo picker can fetch a public metadata list (names and series, no personal data) from its source, and caches it. It can also use a local file instead.
  • Games themselves: a game that tries to reach a service of its own is not given any Napple-run service to talk to.

This website

The site is static and hosted on GitHub Pages. It sets no cookies, runs no analytics or trackers, loads no third-party scripts, fonts or images, and makes no requests other than to its own files. As with any web host, GitHub may keep ordinary server logs of visits (such as IP address) under its own policy; the Napple project does not receive them. Links to GitHub and X lead to their own sites.

Webcam

If a game uses the IR camera feature, macOS asks for camera permission. The picture is used by that game on your Mac and is not sent anywhere by Napple.

Changes

If this changes, this page will say so with a new date. Questions can go to the project on GitHub.
